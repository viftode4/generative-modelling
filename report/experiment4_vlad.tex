\subsection*{Experiment 4 --- Compute-Matched Efficiency: SDXL on Pick-a-Pic (Vlad)}

Z-Sampling performs an additional inversion and re-denoising pass at each step, which
approximately doubles the number of network evaluations per generated image. A fair
assessment of the method therefore requires controlling for total computation rather
than for the number of sampling steps. We reproduce the time-matched efficiency analysis
of the original paper (Figure~10) using Stable Diffusion XL base 1.0 on the first 100
prompts of Pick-a-Pic, with a fixed seed of 42. For each standard step budget
$T_s \in \{20, 30, 40, 50\}$ we compare standard DDIM sampling at $T_s$ steps against a
Z-Sampling configuration with $T_z = T_s/2$ denoising steps and $\lambda = T_s/4$ zigzag
steps. Under this parameterisation the two methods perform the same number of noise
predictions, since $T_z + 2\lambda = T_s$. Both methods are run from identical initial
latents and share the scheduler (DDIM with DDIM inversion), the guidance scales
($\gamma_1 = 5.5$, $\gamma_2 = 0$), and the output resolution ($1024 \times 1024$), so
that the sampling trajectory is the only factor that varies. All 400 image pairs are
scored with HPS\,v2.1, and the per-image generation time is recorded on Apple silicon
(MPS). Because wall-clock measurements on shared hardware are heavy-tailed, we summarise
time by the median rather than the mean.

The results are reported in Table~\ref{tab:vlad-efficiency} and
Figure~\ref{fig:vlad-efficiency}. At each budget the two methods require almost identical
median time per image (for example, 72.2\,s for both at $T_s = 40$), which confirms that
the comparison is compute-matched. Despite this, Z-Sampling attains a higher mean
HPS\,v2.1 score at every budget and is preferred on between 81\% and 91\% of pairs
(85.2\% across all 400 pairs); the mean score difference is small but consistent, and its
95\% bootstrap confidence interval excludes zero at every budget. The advantage also holds
when comparing across budgets. Standard sampling attains its highest score of 0.2727 only
at $T_s = 50$ (median 152.4\,s per image), whereas Z-Sampling exceeds this value already at
$T_s = 30$ (score 0.2779, median 52.7\,s per image), reaching the best standard quality at
roughly one third of the generation time. These findings are consistent with the claim of
the original paper that Z-Sampling can surpass the best standard result while using
substantially less computation. Three limitations apply. The evaluation uses a single seed
and 100 prompts, which is smaller than the original benchmark; the prompts are a publicly
available substitute for the original Pick-a-Pic selection; and absolute runtimes are
hardware-dependent, so the timing should be read only as an internal comparison between the
two methods on identical hardware and initial latents.
