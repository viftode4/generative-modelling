\subsection*{Experiment 4 --- Compute-Matched Efficiency: SDXL on Pick-a-Pic (Vlad)}

Z-Sampling performs an additional inversion and re-denoising pass at each step. In its
default configuration this roughly doubles the number of network evaluations per image,
so a fair assessment must control for total computation rather than for the number of
sampling steps. We reproduce the time-matched efficiency analysis of the original paper
(Figure~10) using Stable Diffusion XL base 1.0 on the first 100 prompts of Pick-a-Pic
with a fixed seed of 42. For each standard step budget $T_s \in \{20, 30, 40, 50\}$ we
compare standard DDIM sampling at $T_s$ steps against a Z-Sampling configuration with
$T_z = T_s/2$ denoising steps and $\lambda = T_s/4$ zigzag steps, which equalises the
noise-prediction count ($T_z + 2\lambda = T_s$). Both methods run from identical initial
latents and share the scheduler (DDIM with DDIM inversion), the guidance scales
($\gamma_1 = 5.5$, $\gamma_2 = 0$) and the output resolution ($1024^2$), so the sampling
trajectory is the only factor that varies. All 400 pairs are scored with HPS\,v2.1, and
the per-image generation time is recorded on Apple silicon (MPS). The sweep ran on a laptop
that was closed and moved during execution, which left a small number of cells with
device-suspend wall-clock artifacts (for example, two $T_s = 50$ standard cells exceeded
3000\,s); we discard any per-arm timing above twice its per-budget median as such an
artifact and report the median of the remainder. Because suspending the process does not
alter the generated image, all 400 pairs are retained for the HPS comparison and only the
timing summary is affected; the remaining per-budget timings are tightly clustered.

The results appear in Table~\ref{tab:vlad-efficiency} and Figure~\ref{fig:vlad-efficiency}.
At each budget the two arms take almost identical median time per image (for example,
72.2\,s for both at $T_s = 40$), so op-count matching does translate into matched
wall-clock on this hardware. Z-Sampling nonetheless attains a higher mean HPS\,v2.1 score
at every budget and is preferred on 81 to 91 percent of pairs (85.2 percent across all
400). The advantage is decisive rather than marginal: the pooled mean delta is $+0.0148$
with a 95 percent bootstrap confidence interval of $[+0.0131, +0.0165]$, larger than the
$+0.0110$ that the same comparison yields for Stable Diffusion 2.1 elsewhere in our study,
and the per-budget interval excludes zero in all four cases. A separate, cross-budget
reading recovers the Pareto result the paper emphasises: standard sampling reaches its
best score, $0.2727$, only at $T_s = 50$ ($\sim$151\,s per image), whereas Z-Sampling
already reaches $0.2779$ at $T_s = 30$ ($\sim$53\,s per image), matching the best standard
quality at roughly one third of the median wall-clock. The paper states this as ``even
with 36\% less computational time, Z-Sampling can surpass the best performance of standard
sampling''; our reimplementation reproduces the direction of that claim. The larger saving
we observe is not directly comparable to the paper given the smaller subset and different
hardware, and the absolute figure is probably an upper bound: the $T_s = 50$ reference
budget runs about twice as long as $T_s = 40$ for only a quarter more steps, suggesting
some thermal throttling on this mobile device that inflates the time the cheaper Z point is
measured against.

Two points qualify this favourable reading. First, ``compute-matched'' equates one
standard and one Z-Sampling noise prediction by count, but the interleaved inversion alters
the scheduler and attention-cache access pattern, so op-count parity is only an
approximation of true cost; our own data show the arms agreeing in the median yet differing
slightly in the mean, and on memory-bound or compiled CUDA stacks the gap could widen.
Second, HPS\,v2.1 is the sole arbiter, and because Z-Sampling explicitly amplifies the
prompt-conditioned guidance signal that preference models are trained to reward, a win
measured only on HPS may partly reflect agreement between the method's objective and the
metric. We follow the paper's Figure~10 protocol, which itself reports HPS\,v2 alone;
corroboration with PickScore, ImageReward, or AES would strengthen the result but lay
outside the compute budget of this study, which ran on a single consumer device (other
experiments in this report do evaluate SDXL under those metrics). With these caveats, the
substantive finding is that Z-Sampling wins at equal compute rather than merely with more
steps: re-investing the budget into $\gamma_1\!\to\!\gamma_2\!\to\!\gamma_1$ self-reflection
cycles on the early, layout-determining timesteps yields more prompt-aligned latents than
spending the same predictions on additional plain denoising, consistent with the paper's
guidance-gap hypothesis (Figure~\ref{fig:vlad-qualitative}). We did not vary the seed,
sweep $\lambda$, or include the resampling baseline in this lane; these are deliberate
scope limits under constrained hardware rather than omissions.
