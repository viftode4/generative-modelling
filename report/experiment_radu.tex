\subsection*{Experiment 6 --- Guidance Gap Ablation: SDXL on Pick-a-Pic (Radu)}

Z-Sampling's inversion pass uses a separate guidance scale $\gamma_2$, forming
a \emph{guidance gap} $\delta = \gamma_1 - \gamma_2$. The paper fixes
$\gamma_1 = 5.5$, $\gamma_2 = 0$, but offers no sensitivity analysis of this
choice. We sweep ten values $\gamma_2 \in \{-20, -10, 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5.5, 7.5, 10\}$
while keeping $\gamma_1 = 5.5$ fixed across 25 Pick-a-Pic prompts (seed~42,
SDXL base~1.0, 50 DDIM steps) and evaluate all images with HPS\,v2, AES,
PickScore, and ImageReward to characterise how output quality depends on this
single hyperparameter.

The ablation reveals a clear regime structure across all four metrics
(Figure~\ref{fig:radu-gap-curve}). A positive gap ($\gamma_2 \in [0,\,4]$)
consistently outperforms the standard baseline (HPS $30.52$, PickScore $22.84$):
HPS\,v2 win rate reaches 83\% at $\gamma_2\!=\!0$ and PickScore peaks at
$\gamma_2\!=\!3$ ($\delta\!=\!2.5$, PickScore $22.88$, win rate $46\%$).
Different metrics favour slightly different gaps --- HPS peaks near $\gamma_2\!=\!0$
while AES climbs monotonically to $6.22$ at $\gamma_2\!=\!5.5$ (gap $= 0$) ---
suggesting that the diversity probe trades prompt-alignment gain (HPS, PickScore)
against aesthetic smoothness (AES). The paper's default $\gamma_2\!=\!0$
represents a reasonable balanced choice within this safe regime, and a small
positive $\gamma_2 \approx 3$ may improve human-preference scores further,
though the margin remains within confidence bounds.

When the gap inverts ($\gamma_2 > \gamma_1\!=\!5.5$), quality collapses
catastrophically and rapidly: at $\gamma_2\!=\!7.5$ ImageReward already falls
to $0.14$, and at $\gamma_2\!=\!10$ mean HPS plummets from $31.4$ to $17.9$
while ImageReward reaches $-1.27$ (Figure~\ref{fig:radu-qualitative}). The
collapse is \emph{asymmetric}: large negative gaps ($\gamma_2\!=\!-20$,
$\delta\!=\!25.5$) degrade all metrics only modestly, because an over-strong
inversion produces over-smoothed latents rather than adversarially misdirected
ones. This asymmetry clarifies the role of $\gamma_2$: during inversion it acts
as a diversity probe, introducing controlled latent perturbation so that
re-denoising can recover a higher-quality layout. Once $\gamma_2$ exceeds
$\gamma_1$, however, the inversion gradient flips sign and actively steers the
latent \emph{away} from the conditioning signal, corrupting the structural
information that the subsequent denoising pass relies on.
